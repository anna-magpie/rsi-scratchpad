--- title: "Returns to software R&D: when does the loop explode?" kind: experiment questions: [1, 2] summary: A one-equation toy model of the software-only feedback loop. Drag r across 1 and watch growth flip from decelerating to finite-time blow-up. ---

Suppose AI fully automates software R&D, so research effort scales with the software level S itself. In the semi-endogenous setup Forethought uses, the returns parameter r says how many doublings of software you get per doubling of cumulative research input. Writing x = log2 S (doublings from today), the loop becomes

dx/dt = g₀ · 2^( β·(r − 1)·x )

where g₀ is today's doublings per year and β is how fast ideas get harder to find. If r > 1 the growth rate rises with every doubling and x blows up in finite time. If r < 1 it slows. r = 1 is a plain exponential. The model has a closed form, so nothing here is simulated:

x(t) = −log₂(1 − k·ln2·g₀·t) / k,   k = β(r − 1)

A ceiling stands in for physical limits: compute, data, and the cost of experiments. Past the ceiling, software-only progress stops.

How likely is r > 1?

Forethought's median is about 1.2 with a range of 0.4–3.6. Reading that range as a 90% interval of a lognormal gives σ ≈ 0.67. The range is symmetric in log space around 1.2, which fits that reading. The shaded area is P(r > 1). It reproduces their "roughly 60%" headline, which is a useful sanity check on the interpretation.

What this does and doesn't show

  • Near r = 1 the answer is extremely sensitive. Moving r from 0.9 to 1.3 changes the qualitative regime, and that is about the width of our uncertainty. This is the CSET point in miniature: data that fits one regime also fits the other.
  • β matters as much as r for timing but not for the sign: acceleration happens iff r > 1, whatever β is.
  • Everything binding outside software (compute growth, experiment latency, human-in-the-loop share) is crushed into the ceiling. A natural next experiment is to make compute a separate, slower-growing input.
  • Forethought call their own model "garbage in, garbage out". This toy is strictly worse. It is for building intuition, not for forecasting.
